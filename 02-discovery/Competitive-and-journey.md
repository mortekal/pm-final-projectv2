# Future State Journey: Fair Way Forward Through Digital Self-Service

**Persona:** A financially stretched consumer with one or more open claims who is anxious about making a mistake.
**Goal:** Commit to a payment plan they can actually sustain so the debt is resolved.
**Friction today:** Faced with a list of plans and no affordability guidance, they pick the shortest term because it "looked like the right answer." The installment is too high and they miss the second payment. Fear ("What if they use it against me?") makes them guess rather than ask.

**Value prop:** Debtors set up a fair installment plan themselves, any time, without calling an agent.

---

## Timeline

```
  (1) ───────────────► (2) ───────────────► (3) ───────────────► (4)
  Enter safely         Share situation      Choose a plan        Confirm and
  [Orient]             [Disclose]           [Decide]             stay on track
                                                                 [Commit]
```

---

## Stages

### 1. Enter safely

- **User action:** Opens one view of all claims plus data-use note -> sees full scope, feels safe.
- **Internal state:** Wary and rushed; worried entered figures will be used against them.
- **Pain point addressed (N-03, N-06):** Single-claim view, data fear -> consolidated picture and upfront reassurance.

### 2. Share situation

- **User action:** Answers clearly worded income question, saves, resumes later -> no lost progress.
- **Internal state:** Hesitant about which income figure counts; relieved by a break-friendly pace.
- **Pain point addressed (N-01, N-06):** Ambiguous income field, no resume -> defined input, saved progress.

### 3. Choose a plan

- **User action:** Compares plans with installment shown against stated income -> picks a sustainable plan.
- **Internal state:** Cautious but guided; stops guessing; sees shortest term is not safest.
- **Pain point addressed (N-02):** Shortest term "looked right" -> affordability guidance prevents unaffordable commitment.

### 4. Confirm and stay on track

- **User action:** Reviews amount, due dates, late-payment consequences, confirms -> no surprises after acceptance.
- **Internal state:** Confident and in control; knows what happens if a payment slips.
- **Pain point addressed (N-05, N-07):** Unclear consequences, mismatched amounts -> one consistent, transparent figure.

---

## Competitive Advantages Over the Manual Workaround

1. **Guided affordability.** Replaces guessing -> plans kept, fewer broken-plan spikes.
2. **Any-time, resumable access.** No phone queue -> completion fits shift-work schedules.
3. **Explainable decisions.** Inputs and rationale recorded -> agents and compliance can explain outcomes.

---

## Notes and Limits

- This is a design hypothesis. The interviews (12 notes, no usage or defect data) support the pain points but not the projected benefits.
- Self-service plan setup already exists (N-02, N-05). The initiative's differentiator is plans people can keep, not simply avoiding the agent.
- Stage 3 is the core of the initiative. How plans are ranked or defaulted is an open design decision the research cannot answer.
- The "Explainable decisions" advantage draws on N-08, N-09 and N-12 (agent and compliance needs), which sit behind the consumer journey rather than in a stage.
