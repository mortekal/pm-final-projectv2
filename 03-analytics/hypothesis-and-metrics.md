# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A financially stretched consumer with one or more open claims who is anxious about making a mistake.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Commit to a payment plan they can actually sustain so the debt is resolved.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** Faced with a list of plans and no affordability guidance, they picked the shortest term because it “looked like the right answer.” The installment was too high and they missed the second payment. Underneath is fear (“What if they use it against me?”) that makes them guess rather than ask.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** Our research documented no external tool or manual process (no app, spreadsheet, or calculator).
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Debtors willing to pay in installments can’t set up a plan without calling an agent, so many default, and clients are moving to partners with fair self-service options.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** Debtors set up a fair installment plan themselves, any time, without calling an agent. That means more plans kept, less default, lower cost-to-collect, and the experience clients now expect. It’s urgent because every month without it risks lost contracts.

## Read the evidence in your case
- **Where does your case say people stall or leave? Does that match the friction you wrote in Module 2?:** 37% of those who reached the affordability questions did not reach plan review
- **What got better or worse in the case, and does that support the workaround your persona uses?:** The user selected the shortest plan, and this is reflected in improved signups but also raised failures on payments rates. agreements rose from 44.2% to 46.0% and missed payments rose from 15.6% to 19.0% of agreements.
- **Which user in the case is carrying the pain, and is that the person your Module 2 persona describes?:** This matches my Module 2 persona in part. N-03 had two open claims, N-01 did shift work, and N-06 had three claims. The data shows both groups struggle most

## Step 2 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** I picked the plan with the lowest number of months because it looked like the right answer. The amount was too high and I missed the second payment.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** missed-payment rate rose from 15.6% (Month 3) to 19.0% (Month 6) while the agreement rate rose from 44.2% to 46.0%
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Role: A financially stretched consumer with one or more open claims who is anxious about making a mistake.
Goal: Commit to a payment plan they can sustain so the debt is resolved.
Friction confirmed: The plan choice without affordability guidance, which the Month 4 change supports. The multi-claim and variable-income segments also support it. The data cannot confirm the anxiety, because the pack has no hardship or stress measure.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Consumers who start an installment plan online are shown the shortest term first with no affordability guidance, so they commit to installments they can’t sustain and miss payments.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Consumers choose plans that match what they can pay.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Plans kept per journey starter
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Agreement rate must stay at or above 44.2%
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Run two monthly cohorts (about 8,300 starters each), then wait for the 60-day missed-payment reading on the second cohort, so roughly 4 months in total.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on “it looked like the right answer… I missed the second payment” and missed payments rising from 15.6% to 19.0%, I believe guiding plan choice by affordability for financially stretched consumers with open claims will result in more plans kept, as measured by a 1.5-point rise in plans kept per journey starter. I will protect the agreement rate at or above 44.2% and decide go/no-go after about 4 months .
